Apple was founded on April 1, 1976, by Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, and Ronald Wayne in Cupertino, California. It has grown into one of the world's most valuable and recognizable technology companies, known for iconic products such as the Macintosh computer, the iPod, the iPhone, the iPad, and the Mac. Apple operates a tightly integrated ecosystem of hardware, software, and services — including the App Store, iCloud, Apple Music, and Apple Pay — and is renowned for its emphasis on design, user experience, and privacy. As of the mid-2020s, Apple consistently ranks among the top companies by market capitalization globally, with operations spanning retail stores, online services, and a vast supply chain across dozens of countries.

The apple (Malus domestica) is a deciduous fruit tree in the rose family (Rosaceae), originally cultivated in Central Asia and now grown worldwide. It produces sweet to tart round fruits with a thin edible skin, crisp flesh, and a core containing seeds. Apples come in thousands of cultivated varieties — including Fuji, Gala, Granny Smith, and Honeycrisp — differing in color, flavor, size, and texture. Rich in dietary fiber, vitamin C, and various antioxidants, apples are associated with numerous health benefits and are commonly consumed fresh, juiced, or used in cooking and baking. The fruit holds deep cultural significance across many civilizations, appearing in mythology, religion, and literature, from the biblical garden of Eden to Newton's legendary encounter with gravity.
